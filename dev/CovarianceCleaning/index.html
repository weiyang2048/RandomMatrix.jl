Covariance Cleaning

A sample covariance matrix overstates the large eigenvalues and understates the small ones: its spectrum is spread out by the Marčenko–Pastur law even when the population covariance is the identity. Cleaning shrinks the eigenvalues (keeping the sample eigenvectors) toward a fitted noise model.

  • Noise-level fit — estimate $\sigma^2$ from the bulk of the spectrum.
  • Eigenvalue clipping (Laloux et al.) — replace bulk eigenvalues by their average, keep the outliers; trace-preserving.
  • Rotationally invariant estimator (Bun, Bouchaud & Potters, whose shrinkage function is the one Ledoit–Wolf analytic nonlinear shrinkage also estimates) — a smooth, asymptotically Frobenius-optimal shrinkage.
  • Linear shrinkage from the data matrix (Ledoit–Wolf 2004; OAS, Chen et al. 2010) — shrink the sample covariance itself toward a scaled identity, with a data-estimated intensity; see the last section.

Finite-size note: the RIE broadening

The RIE evaluates the empirical Stieltjes transform at $z_i = \lambda_i - i\eta$ with default broadening $\eta = p^{-1/2}$. At moderate $p$ this leaves a positive bias in the mean cleaned eigenvalue under the null ($\Sigma = I$): the broadening smooths the spectrum over a window of $O(\sqrt p)$ eigenvalues, and the bias is of the order of $\eta$ itself. Measured means of $\mathrm{mean}(\xi)$ over 24 seeded Wishart trials:

$p$$\gamma = 0.25$, $\eta = p^{-1/2}$$\gamma = 0.5$, $\eta = p^{-1/2}$$\gamma = 0.5$, $\eta = p^{-2/3}$
1001.0721.1271.050
2001.0521.0921.032
4001.0361.0661.019

Shrinking $\eta$ cuts the bias roughly threefold at $\eta = p^{-2/3}$, but $\eta$ cannot approach 0 at fixed $p$: the self-term $1/(z_i - \lambda_i)$ is what regularizes the transform at its own pole, and below the mean eigenvalue spacing the estimator deteriorates. On a spiked population ($\gamma = 0.5$, Frobenius loss in oracle units, 8 trials) the default is near-optimal — 1.21 / 1.15 / 1.14 at $p = 100 / 200 / 400$, against 1.30 / 1.15 / 1.09 for $\eta = p^{-2/3}$ and 1.56 / 1.32 / 1.21 for $\eta = p^{-3/4}$ — so the default stands. Pass a smaller eta to rieEigenvalues when an accurate trace matters more than minimum loss, in particular at $p \ge 200$.

RandomMatrix.fitMarchenkoPastur — Function
fitMarchenkoPastur(evals, gamma; method=:trim)

Estimate the noise variance sigma^2 of a covariance spectrum by fitting the Marcenko-Pastur law with aspect ratio gamma = p/n to the eigenvalues evals (the eigenvalues of a sample covariance matrix).

Methods:

  • :trim (default): iterate sigma^2 <- mean{lambda_i <= sigma^2 (1+sqrt(gamma))^2} to a fixed point - eigenvalues above the fitted upper edge are treated as signal and excluded from the noise estimate.
  • :median: match medians, sigma^2 = median(evals) / median(MP_gamma,1). Requires gamma < 2: for gamma >= 2 the Marcenko-Pastur median is 0 (the atom at 0 has mass 1 - 1/gamma >= 1/2), so median matching is degenerate and an ArgumentError is thrown; use :trim there.

RandomMatrix.clipEigenvalues — Function
clipEigenvalues(evals, gamma; sigma2=fitMarchenkoPastur(evals, gamma))

Eigenvalue clipping (Laloux et al. 1999): eigenvalues inside the fitted Marcenko-Pastur bulk (lambda <= sigma^2 (1+sqrt(gamma))^2) are replaced by their average; eigenvalues above the edge (signal) are kept unchanged. The replacement preserves the trace exactly. Returns a vector aligned with the input order.

RandomMatrix.rieEigenvalues — Function
rieEigenvalues(evals, gamma; eta=length(evals)^(-1/2))

Cleaned eigenvalues of the rotationally invariant estimator (Bun, Bouchaud & Potters 2017; the Ledoit-Peche formula):

xi_i = lambda_i / |1 - gamma + gamma * lambda_i * g(z_i)|^2,
z_i = lambda_i - i*eta,
g(z) = (1/p) * sum_j 1 / (z - lambda_j)

with gamma = p/n and g the empirical Stieltjes transform of the sample spectrum, broadened by eta (a Lorentzian local average over neighbouring eigenvalues). The keyword is also accepted under its Greek spelling η (eta takes precedence when both are given). Properties: for a pure-noise spectrum (Sigma = sigma^2 I) the result is flat at sigma^2 asymptotically; a supercritical spike l observed at lambda > l is shrunk to xi < l < lambda.

Finite-size bias: at moderate p the default broadening eta = p^(-1/2) leaves a positive bias in the mean cleaned eigenvalue under the null (Sigma = I). Simulation means of mean(xi) (24 trials) at gamma = 0.5 are about 1.13, 1.09 and 1.07 at p = 100, 200, 400 (1.07, 1.05, 1.04 at gamma = 0.25) — a bias of roughly c(gamma) * p^(-1/2) with c(0.25) ≈ 0.73 and c(0.5) ≈ 1.3, i.e. of the order of eta itself. The mechanism is the broadening: g is evaluated a distance eta off the real axis, which smooths the spectrum over a window containing O(sqrt(p)) eigenvalues. Shrinking eta reduces the bias — at eta = p^(-2/3) the null means above drop to about 1.05, 1.03, 1.02 — but eta cannot be taken to zero at fixed p: the self-term 1/(z_i - lambda_i) = 1/(-i*eta) is what regularizes g at its own pole, and once eta reaches the mean eigenvalue spacing the estimator deteriorates (on a spiked population its Frobenius loss, in oracle units, grows from about 1.15 at the default to about 1.33 at eta = p^(-3/4), p = 200, gamma = 0.5). The default is kept because it is near loss-optimal in those spiked simulations; pass a smaller eta (e.g. eta = length(evals)^(-2/3)) when an accurate trace matters more than minimum loss — at p >= 200 its spiked loss is tied with or better than the default, while at p = 100 it costs about 8%.

Requires gamma < 1 for the zero-eigenvalue block when gamma >= 1: eigenvalues equal to 0 are mapped to 0 (see cleanCovariance).

RandomMatrix.cleanCovariance — Function
cleanCovariance(S, n; method=:rie)

Clean the sample covariance matrix S (formed from n observations) by eigenvalue shrinkage in its own eigenbasis: with gamma = size(S,1)/n,

  • method=:rie - the rotationally invariant estimator (rieEigenvalues); requires n >= size(S, 1).
  • method=:clip - eigenvalue clipping (clipEigenvalues) with the noise level fitted by fitMarchenkoPastur.

Returns a Symmetric matrix with the sample eigenvectors and the cleaned eigenvalues.

RandomMatrix.ledoitWolf — Function
ledoitWolf(X; target = :identity)

Ledoit-Wolf linear shrinkage estimate of the covariance matrix from the data matrix X (p x n: variables in rows, observations in columns; the data are centered internally):

Sigma_hat = (1 - rho) * S + rho * F,

where S is the sample covariance (1/n divisor), F is the shrinkage target selected by target, and rho is the asymptotically optimal shrinkage intensity estimated from the data (ledoitWolfShrinkage). Two targets are available:

  • target = :identity (the default; Ledoit & Wolf 2004): the scaled identity F = m * I with m = tr(S) / p.
  • target = :constant_correlation (Ledoit & Wolf 2003): the constant-correlation matrix, which keeps the sample variances on the diagonal and sets every off-diagonal entry to rbar * sqrt(s_ii * s_jj), where rbar is the average of the sample correlations. The estimate then preserves the diagonal of S (hence its trace) exactly.

Shrinking toward a structured target trades a little bias for a large variance reduction; the estimate is positive definite whenever rho > 0 and the target is, even for p > n. Unlike cleanCovariance, which works from a covariance matrix and shrinks its eigenvalues nonlinearly, this estimator needs the data themselves, because the intensity estimate involves fourth moments of the observations.

Note this is the linear shrinkage estimator, not the Ledoit-Wolf (2020) analytic nonlinear shrinkage whose shrinkage function rieEigenvalues implements.

References: Ledoit & Wolf, "Improved estimation of the covariance matrix of stock returns with an application to portfolio selection", Journal of Empirical Finance 10 (2003), 603-621; Ledoit & Wolf, "A well-conditioned estimator for large-dimensional covariance matrices", Journal of Multivariate Analysis 88 (2004), 365-411.

RandomMatrix.ledoitWolfShrinkage — Function
ledoitWolfShrinkage(X; target = :identity)

The Ledoit-Wolf shrinkage intensity rho in [0, 1] used by ledoitWolf with the same target, for the data matrix X (p x n, centered internally).

For target = :identity, with S the sample covariance, m = tr(S)/p and the centered observations x_k,

rho = min(bbar^2, d^2) / d^2,
d^2    = ||S - mI||_F^2 / p,
bbar^2 = ( (1/n) * sum_k ||x_k||^4 - ||S||_F^2 ) / (n * p),

where bbar^2 is (1/(n^2 p)) * sum_k ||x_k x_k' - S||_F^2 with the cross terms summed in closed form. The values agree with scikit-learn's ledoit_wolf (cross-checked to 1e-15 on fixed datasets). If S is exactly a multiple of the identity, every intensity yields the same estimator and rho = 1 (the limit from non-degenerate data) is returned by convention; scikit-learn returns 0 in that corner, with the same shrunk matrix.

For target = :constant_correlation, the intensity is the formula Ledoit & Wolf (2003) derive for that target. With F the constant-correlation target, rbar the average sample correlation, and the entrywise asymptotic variances and covariances

pi_ij      = (1/n) * sum_k (x_ik x_jk - s_ij)^2,
theta_ii,ij = (1/n) * sum_k (x_ik^2 - s_ii) (x_ik x_jk - s_ij),

the estimator is

rho = clamp( (pi - rhohat) / (gamma * n), 0, 1 ),
pi    = sum_ij pi_ij,
rhohat = sum_i pi_ii + sum_{i != j} (rbar / 2) *
         ( sqrt(s_jj / s_ii) * theta_ii,ij
           + sqrt(s_ii / s_jj) * theta_jj,ij ),
gamma = ||S - F||_F^2.

Here pi sums the asymptotic variances of the entries of S, rhohat sums their asymptotic covariances with the entries of the (random) target F — the diagonal contributes pi_ii because f_ii = s_ii, and each off-diagonal covariance follows from the delta method applied to f_ij = rbar * sqrt(s_ii s_jj) — and gamma measures the target's misspecification. Unlike the identity formula this is not scikit-learn's estimator (which only implements the identity target); the values are cross-checked against an independent implementation of the papers' formulas. Pairs touching a zero-variance variable contribute 0 to rbar and to the rhohat double sum (their theta terms vanish identically). If gamma = 0 — S already equals its constant-correlation target, always the case for p = 1 — every intensity gives the same estimator and rho = 1 by the same limit convention as above.

RandomMatrix.oas — Function
oas(X)

Oracle approximating shrinkage (OAS) estimate of the covariance matrix from the data matrix X (p x n: variables in rows, observations in columns; the data are centered internally):

Sigma_hat = (1 - rho) * S + rho * m * I,   m = tr(S) / p,

with S the sample covariance (1/n divisor) and rho the OAS intensity (oasShrinkage). OAS refines the Ledoit-Wolf linear shrinkage under Gaussian assumptions by approximating the oracle (MMSE-optimal) intensity with the population traces replaced by iterated estimates; in small samples it typically shrinks noticeably more than ledoitWolf. Like it, the estimate preserves the trace of S exactly and is positive definite whenever rho > 0, even for p > n.

Reference: Chen, Wiesel, Eldar & Hero, "Shrinkage algorithms for MMSE covariance estimation", IEEE Transactions on Signal Processing 58(10) (2010), 5016-5029.

source
RandomMatrix.oasShrinkage — Function
oasShrinkage(X)

The oracle approximating shrinkage (OAS) intensity rho in [0, 1] used by oas, for the data matrix X (p x n, centered internally). With S the sample covariance (1/n divisor),

rho = ( (1 - 2/p) * tr(S^2) + tr(S)^2 ) /
      ( (n + 1 - 2/p) * (tr(S^2) - tr(S)^2 / p) ),

clipped to [0, 1] — Eq. (23) of Chen, Wiesel, Eldar & Hero (2010), with the 2/p terms retained. (scikit-learn's oas omits those terms, as its documentation notes; at small p that changes rho by several percent, e.g. 0.88 instead of 0.79 on a p = 4, n = 6 test dataset.) If S is exactly a multiple of the identity the denominator vanishes; every intensity then gives the same estimator and rho = 1, the continuous limit, is returned.

Worked example

Cleaning a spiked sample covariance ($p = 100$, $n = 400$, one population spike at $\ell = 4$): the noise fit recovers $\sigma^2 \approx 1$, clipping collapses the 99 bulk eigenvalues to a single value while keeping the outlier, and the RIE shrinks the outlier back toward the population spike.

using RandomMatrix, Random, LinearAlgebra, Statistics
Random.seed!(20261030)
p, n = 100, 400
gamma = p / n
S = randSpikedCovariance(p, n, [4.0])
ev = eigvals(S)
(fit = round(fitMarchenkoPastur(ev, gamma), digits = 3),
 raw_top = round(ev[end], digits = 3))
(fit = 1.01, raw_top = 4.077)
clipped = clipEigenvalues(ev, gamma)
(n_distinct = length(unique(clipped)), top = round(maximum(clipped), digits = 3))
(n_distinct = 2, top = 4.077)
rie = rieEigenvalues(ev, gamma)
(top = round(maximum(rie), digits = 3), mean = round(mean(rie), digits = 3))
(top = 3.4, mean = 1.109)

The RIE mean above 1 is the finite-size broadening bias quantified in the previous section (about $1.09$ expected at $\gamma = 0.5$; here $\gamma = 0.25$ and the spike also contributes).

Linear shrinkage from the data matrix

The estimators above start from a covariance matrix (or its spectrum). The Ledoit–Wolf (2004) and OAS (Chen, Wiesel, Eldar & Hero 2010) estimators instead start from the data matrix $X$ ($p \times n$, observations in columns) and shrink the sample covariance linearly toward the scaled identity $mI$, $m = \mathrm{tr}(S)/p$:

  • Ledoit–Wolf — the intensity minimizes the asymptotic Frobenius risk; its estimate involves fourth moments of the observations, which is why the raw data (not just $S$) are needed.
  • OAS — the oracle-approximating refinement under Gaussian assumptions, Eq. (23) of Chen et al. with the $2/p$ terms retained (scikit-learn's oas omits them); it typically shrinks more than Ledoit–Wolf at small $n$.

Both preserve the trace of $S$ exactly and are positive definite whenever the intensity is positive — in particular for $p > n$, where $S$ itself is singular.

Simulated data ($p = 100$, $n = 150$) from a diagonal population with three large variances, the rest at $1$: both estimators cut the sample covariance's Frobenius loss against the population, and lift the smallest eigenvalue away from the Marčenko–Pastur lower edge.

using RandomMatrix, Random, LinearAlgebra, Statistics
Random.seed!(20261030)
p, n = 100, 150
d = vcat([8.0, 4.0, 2.0], fill(1.0, p - 3))
X = Diagonal(sqrt.(d)) * randn(p, n)
Xc = X .- mean(X; dims = 2)
S = (Xc * Xc') / n
loss(M) = round(sum(abs2, M - Diagonal(d)), digits = 2)
(sample = loss(S), ledoit_wolf = loss(Matrix(ledoitWolf(X))),
 oas = loss(Matrix(oas(X))))
(sample = 82.91, ledoit_wolf = 42.33, oas = 42.45)
(lw_rho = round(ledoitWolfShrinkage(X), digits = 3),
 oas_rho = round(oasShrinkage(X), digits = 3),
 min_eig_sample = round(eigmin(S), digits = 3),
 min_eig_lw = round(eigmin(ledoitWolf(X)), digits = 3),
 min_eig_oas = round(eigmin(oas(X)), digits = 3))
(lw_rho = 0.7, oas_rho = 0.706, min_eig_sample = 0.035, min_eig_lw = 0.762, min_eig_oas = 0.768)
