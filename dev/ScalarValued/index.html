Wachter Law

RandomMatrix.Wachter — Type
Wachter(γ₁, γ₂)
Wachter()

The Wachter law (the MANOVA / Jacobi limiting spectral distribution): the limiting distribution of an eigenvalue of $(A + B)^{-1} A$, where $A \sim W_p(I, n_1)$ and $B \sim W_p(I, n_2)$ are independent Wishart matrices whose degrees of freedom grow proportionally with $p$, with aspect ratios

\[\gamma_1 = p / n_1, \qquad \gamma_2 = p / n_2\]

(the same convention as MarchenkoPastur, whose $\lambda$ is $p / n$). With $\rho = \sqrt{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2 - \gamma_1 \gamma_2}$ the continuous part has density

\[f(x) = \frac{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}{\gamma_1 \gamma_2} \, \frac{\sqrt{(x_+ - x)(x - x_-)}}{2\pi x (1 - x)}, \qquad x \in [x_-, x_+],\]

on the support $x_\pm = \gamma_2 (\gamma_1 \pm \rho)^2 / (\gamma_1 + \gamma_2)^2$. If $\gamma_1 > 1$ (the numerator Wishart is rank-deficient) there is an atom at $0$ of mass $1 - 1/\gamma_1$; if $\gamma_2 > 1$ there is an atom at $1$ of mass $1 - 1/\gamma_2$. Following the convention of MarchenkoPastur, pdf at an atom location returns the atom mass. The mean and variance are

\[\frac{\gamma_2}{\gamma_1 + \gamma_2}, \qquad \frac{(\gamma_1 \gamma_2)^2}{(\gamma_1 + \gamma_2)^3},\]

and $1 - X$ for $X \sim$ Wachter(γ₁, γ₂) is Wachter(γ₂, γ₁) (the roles of the two Wisharts swap). The parameters must satisfy $\gamma_1, \gamma_2 > 0$ and $\rho^2 = \gamma_1 + \gamma_2 - \gamma_1\gamma_2 > 0$, equivalently $n_1 + n_2 > p$ (so that $A + B$ is invertible). Defaults are γ₁ = γ₂ = 0.5, the symmetric case with mean 1/2.

rand uses exact rejection sampling in the variable $x = x_- + (x_+ - x_-) \sin^2\theta$, in which the density is bounded; cdf integrates the density in the same variable (Simpson's rule), in which it is smooth even when a support edge touches 0 or 1.

Examples

d = Wachter(0.25, 0.5)   # eigenvalues of (A + B)^{-1} A, p/n1 = 1/4, p/n2 = 1/2
mean(d)                  # 0.666...
pdf(d, 0.5)              # 1.2732...
rand(d)                  # a draw in [0, 1]

References: Wachter, "The limiting empirical measure of multiple discriminant ratios", Ann. Statist. 8(5), 1980, 937–957; Collins, "Product of random projections, Jacobi ensembles and universality problems arising from free probability", Probab. Theory Related Fields 133, 2005, 315–349; Johnstone, "Multivariate analysis and Jacobi ensembles: Largest eigenvalue, Tracy–Widom limits and rates of convergence", Ann. Statist. 36(6), 2008, 2638–2716.

Worked example

The symmetric case Wachter(0.5, 0.5) has mean $1/2$ by the $\gamma_2 / (\gamma_1 + \gamma_2)$ formula, and its cdf at the mean is exactly $1/2$ by the swap symmetry $1 - X \sim$ Wachter(γ₂, γ₁). With a rank-deficient numerator Wishart ($\gamma_1 = 1.5 > 1$) a third of the mass sits in the atom at 0, while the continuous part still carries the rest of the mean:

using RandomMatrix, Distributions
d = Wachter(0.5, 0.5)
(mean(d), var(d), round(cdf(d, 0.5), digits = 6))
(0.5, 0.0625, 0.5)
using RandomMatrix, Distributions
d = Wachter(1.5, 0.5)
(pdf(d, 0), mean(d), round(cdf(d, 0.999), digits = 6)) # atom 1/3 at 0; no atom at 1
(0.33333333333333337, 0.25, 1.0)

Stieltjes Transform of the Semicircle Law

RandomMatrix.stieltjes — Function
stieltjes(d::Semicircle)

Return the Stieltjes transform of the semicircle law d as a function of one argument z:

\[m(z) = \frac{-z + \sqrt{z^2 - r^2}}{2}, \qquad r = d.r .\]

Examples

stieltjes(Semicircle(2))(0)  # == im
