Free Probability

Free additive ($\boxplus$) and multiplicative ($\boxtimes$) convolution of probability measures, computed numerically. A FreeMeasure is a discrete representation (nodes and weights) of a probability measure; the Cauchy, R and S transforms are then exact rational functions, and the convolutions are evaluated by the subordination fixed point (additive case) and the S-transform product rule (multiplicative case). Atoms of a convolution follow the exact Bercovici–Voiculescu rules. The page also covers the package's first non-Hermitian limiting law, the Brown measure of an R-diagonal element, computed from the same S-transform machinery.

Accuracy notes

freePoissonLaw is discretized on a Chebyshev grid, which resolves the density spike at the lower Marčenko–Pastur edge for gamma near 1 (on a uniform grid the mean came out 2.3% high at gamma = 0.99, and at gamma = 1 the density at 0 is NaN, which poisoned every weight); its mean and variance now match 1 and gamma to about 1e-7.

For freeMul, measured against the exact moments of MP_gamma ⊠ MP_delta (from the S-transform) at the default resolution: with both factors bounded away from 0, the first three moments err by at most about 2.5%. When a factor's support touches 0, the product density diverges at 0, the Stieltjes smoothing (eps, default half the mean grid spacing) leaks mass below 0 at a rate ~ eps^(1/3), and the errors grow to about 5% (MP_1 ⊠ MP_0.5) and 11% (MP_1 ⊠ MP_1). A smaller eps — down to about 1e-4 times the support scale — improves the atom-free cases substantially, but is not recommended for measures with an atom at 0, where the Newton continuation fails near the lower edge.

Worked example

The free Poisson (Marčenko–Pastur) law has mean $1$ and variance $\gamma$, reproduced here to the accuracy quoted above; the free multiplicative convolution of two such laws has exact mean $1 \cdot 1 = 1$ and exact second moment $1 + \gamma + \delta$ (S-transform), hence variance $\gamma + \delta = 0.5$ for $\gamma = \delta = 0.25$ — the numerical convolution lands within about 1% of both, as expected for factors bounded away from 0.

using RandomMatrix
fp = freePoissonLaw(0.5)
(mean = round(freeMean(fp), digits = 6), var = round(freeVar(fp), digits = 6))
(mean = 1.0, var = 0.5)
prod = freeMul(freePoissonLaw(0.25), freePoissonLaw(0.25))
(mean = round(freeMean(prod), digits = 3), var = round(freeVar(prod), digits = 3))
(mean = 1.002, var = 0.503)

Free additive convolution of two semicircles of variance 1 is a semicircle of variance 2 (the R-transforms add):

ss = freeAdd(semicircleLaw(1.0), semicircleLaw(1.0))
(mean = round(freeMean(ss), digits = 3), var = round(freeVar(ss), digits = 3))
(mean = -0.0, var = 2.015)

Free compression by a projection of trace $\tau$ (the law of $p a p$ in the compressed space, with the trace renormalized by $1/\tau$) scales the R-transform as $R(w) \mapsto R(\tau w)$; in particular the Marčenko–Pastur law $MP_\gamma$ compresses to $MP_{\tau\gamma}$ — the limiting spectral law of a principal submatrix retaining a $\tau$ fraction of the coordinates. Here $MP_{0.5}$ compressed by $\tau = 1/2$ lands on the mean and variance of $MP_{0.25}$:

comp = freeCompress(freePoissonLaw(0.5), 0.5)
(mean = round(freeMean(comp), digits = 3), var = round(freeVar(comp), digits = 3))
(mean = 1.001, var = 0.252)

General populations: the Silverstein equation

For a sample covariance $(1/n) T^{1/2} X X' T^{1/2}$ whose population spectral distribution $H$ is not a point mass, silversteinLaw computes the limiting law $F$ from the Silverstein equation — the companion Stieltjes transform $\underline m$ is the unique upper-half-plane solution of $\underline m = -(z - \gamma \int t/(1 + t\underline m)\, dH(t))^{-1}$, with $\underline m(z) = -(1-\gamma)/z + \gamma m_F(z)$ recovering the transform of $F$ itself. The exact moments are $\int x\,dF = \int t\,dH$ and $\int x^2 dF = \int t^2 dH + \gamma(\int t\,dH)^2$; below, a two-point population with mean $2.5$ and second moment $8.5$ at $\gamma = 0.5$ has exact (mean, variance) $(2.5,\ 8.5 + 0.5 \cdot 2.5^2 - 2.5^2) = (2.5,\ 5.375)$, and the solver lands within its calibrated ~0.3% of both:

sg = silversteinLaw([1.0, 4.0], [0.5, 0.5], 0.5)
(mean = round(freeMean(sg), digits = 3), var = round(freeVar(sg), digits = 3))
(mean = 2.501, var = 5.378)

At $\gamma = 0.1$ the same population's support splits into two clusters (the density at the former gap midpoint $x = 1.95$ is essentially zero, against $0.74$ inside the first cluster):

sg2 = silversteinLaw([1.0, 4.0], [0.5, 0.5], 0.1)
round.(freeDensity(sg2, [0.9, 1.95, 4.0]), digits = 3)
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.74
 0.003
 0.179

Free Bessel laws

FreeBessel(s, t) is the free Bessel law $\pi_{s,t}$ of Banica, Belinschi, Capitaine & Collins (Canad. J. Math. 63(1), 2011): the law whose moment series satisfies $f = 1 + z f^s (f + t - 1)$. At $t = 1$ and integer $s$ it is the limiting spectral distribution of a product of $s$ independent square Ginibre (equivalently square Wishart) matrices — $\pi_{2,1}$ is the free square of the Marčenko–Pastur law — and its moments are the Fuss–Catalan numbers. For $t < 1$ it carries an atom $1 - t$ at 0 and its support is bounded away from 0. The parametrization is $(s, t)$ as in the paper, deliberately not a product depth and a Marčenko–Pastur ratio: a product of $k \ge 2$ laws $\mathrm{MP}_\gamma$ with $\gamma \ne 1$ is $\pi_{s,t}$ for no $(s, t)$. Exact moments (freeBesselMoments, the Fuss–Narayana formula) and the support and atom (freeBesselSupport) are separate queries; the constructor returns the grid law itself, whose first four moments match the exact ones to at most about 0.13% at the default resolution.

fb = FreeBessel(2, 1)             # product of two square Wisharts' limit
(mean = round(freeMean(fb), digits = 3), var = round(freeVar(fb), digits = 3))
(mean = 1.0, var = 2.0)
freeBesselMoments(2, 1, 5)        # Fuss-Catalan: 1, 3, 12, 55, 273
5-element Vector{Float64}:
   1.0
   3.0
  12.0
  55.0
 273.0
freeAtoms(FreeBessel(2, 0.5))     # atom 1 - t at 0
1-element Vector{Pair{Float64, Float64}}:
 0.0 => 0.5

Variance profiles: the quadratic vector equation

For a Wigner matrix whose entry variances vary across the matrix, $\mathbb{E}|X_{ij}|^2 = S_{ij}/n$ for a deterministic profile $S$, the limiting Stieltjes transform is no longer a scalar: the vector $m(z)$ solves the quadratic vector equation $-1/m_i = z + \frac{1}{n}\sum_j S_{ij} m_j$ on the upper half plane, and the limiting density is the average of the component densities (Ajanki–Erdős–Krüger). profileWignerLaw solves the QVE and returns the law as a FreeMeasure; randProfileWigner samples the ensemble. The constant profile is the semicircle; structured profiles give laws no single classical object covers. Two communities with within-variances 2 and 1 and cross-variance 0.5 form a single cluster whose variance is the mean profile entry, (2 + 1 + 2\cdot 0.5)/4 = 1:

mu = profileWignerLaw([100, 100], [2.0 0.5; 0.5 1.0])
round(freeVar(mu), digits = 4)
1.0001
round.(freeDensity(mu, [-1.0, 0.0, 1.0]), digits = 3)
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.272
 0.328
 0.272

A block-diagonal profile is an exact mixture of semicircles, with the full-size normalization folding the block fraction into the radius: a block of variance b on a fraction p of the coordinates contributes radius 2\sqrt{bp} with weight p. Here $0.6\,\mathrm{SC}(\sqrt{1.35}) + 0.4\,\mathrm{SC}(\sqrt{0.256})$:

mix = profileWignerLaw([60, 40], [2.25 0.0; 0.0 0.64])
round.(freeDensity(mix, [0.0, 0.5, 2.0]), digits = 3)
3-element Vector{Float64}:
 0.413
 0.376
 0.083

A coordinate with an identically zero profile row is an exact zero eigenvalue of every realization; such rows leave the solve and come back as an exact atom at 0:

Sz = fill(1.0, 30, 30)
Sz[7, :] .= 0.0
Sz[:, 7] .= 0.0
freeAtoms(profileWignerLaw(Sz))
1-element Vector{Pair{Float64, Float64}}:
 0.0 => 0.03333333333333333

Random features (Mei–Montanari)

For a random-features map $\Phi = \sigma(W X / \sqrt d)$ with $W$ ($m \times d$) and $X$ ($d \times n$) i.i.d. Gaussian, randomFeaturesLaw computes the limiting spectral law of the feature covariance $\Phi \Phi' / n$. The activation enters only through its Hermite coefficients $(\mu_0, \mu_1, \mu_\star)$ for $Z \sim N(0,1)$ (hermiteCoefficients): the bulk law is the Silverstein law above, with the affine Marchenko–Pastur population $H = \mathrm{law}(\mu_1^2 T + \mu_\star^2)$, $T \sim MP_{m/d}$, at aspect $\gamma = m/n$ — equivalently, the solution of the Pennington–Worah quartic self-consistent equation. It reduces exactly to a scaled Marchenko–Pastur law when $\mu_1 = 0$, and to the product-Wishart law $MP_{m/d} \boxtimes MP_{m/n}$ for a linear activation. $\mu_0$ does not enter the bulk; it produces a single outlier at $\mu_0^2 m (1 + o(1))$. For ReLU features at $n/d = 1.5$, $m/d = 1.25$ the mean is $\mu_1^2 + \mu_\star^2 = 0.3408$ (the solver lands ~0.6% above it at the default regularization):

F = randomFeaturesLaw(:relu, 1.5, 1.25)
(mean = round(freeMean(F), digits = 3), var = round(freeVar(F), digits = 3))
(mean = 0.343, var = 0.176)

Non-Hermitian: the Brown measure (single ring law)

For an R-diagonal element $a$ — equivalently $a = up$ with $u$ a Haar unitary free from a positive $p$ — the Brown measure $\mu_a$ is the two-dimensional limiting eigenvalue distribution. It is rotation invariant, so a radial law determines it, and Haagerup & Larsen compute that law from $\nu$, the distribution of $a^*a$, through its S-transform (J. Funct. Anal. 176 (2000), 331–367, Thm. 4.4, Cor. 4.5): $\mu_a(B(0, S_\nu(t-1)^{-1/2})) = t$ for $t \in (\nu(\{0\}), 1]$. The support is the annulus (the single ring) with exact radii $\sqrt{\int t\,d\nu}$ (outer) and $\sqrt{1/\int t^{-1}\,d\nu}$ (inner, 0 when $\nu$ has an atom at 0 or $\int t^{-1} d\nu = \infty$), and $\mu_a$ carries an atom at 0 of mass exactly $\nu(\{0\})$. Guionnet, Krishnapur & Zeitouni prove that the eigenvalues of $A = UTV$ ($U, V$ Haar unitary, $T$ deterministic diagonal with $T^2$'s spectral law converging to $\nu$, $\|T\|$ bounded, no singular-value mass escaping to 0) converge to this measure (Ann. of Math. 174(2) (2011), 1189–1217).

brownMeasure takes $\nu$ in the same three forms as silversteinLaw and returns a BrownMeasure — the radii, the atom at 0, and the radial CDF and planar density $\rho(z) = \Phi'(|z|)/(2\pi|z|)$ on a grid — since a real-line FreeMeasure cannot represent a planar law. Read it with radialCDF and brownDensity. Boundary cases are exact: $\nu = \delta_c$ gives the uniform measure on the circle of radius $\sqrt c$ (singular: the density grid is empty), and an atom of $\nu$ at 0 reappears as the atom at 0.

bm = brownMeasure([1.0, 4.0], [0.5, 0.5])
(round(bm.inner, digits = 4), round(bm.outer, digits = 4), radialCDF(bm, sqrt(2.0)))
(1.2649, 1.5811, 0.49999999999999994)

The radii are $\sqrt{1.6}$ and $\sqrt{2.5}$, and the median radius is exactly $\sqrt 2$. Haagerup & Larsen's Bernoulli example, $\nu = \tfrac12\delta_0 + \tfrac12\delta_1$, has radial CDF $\tfrac12/(1-r^2)$ and density $\tfrac12/(\pi(1-r^2)^2)$ on the disk of radius $\sqrt{1/2}$:

bb = brownMeasure([0.0, 1.0], [0.5, 0.5])
(bb.atom0, round(radialCDF(bb, 0.4), digits = 4), round(brownDensity(bb, 0.4), digits = 4))
(0.5, 0.5952, 0.2256)

The circular element ($\nu =$MP$_1$, via brownMeasure(freePoissonLaw(1.0))) is the uniform unit disk: radial CDF $r^2$ to about $10^{-7}$ and density $1/\pi$ on the bulk of the disk. Formed-matrix simulation ($A = UP$ at $n = 500$) matches the radial CDF with pooled KS distance about $0.03$, the deviation sitting in the finite-$n$ edge layers.

RandomMatrix.freePoissonLaw — Function
freePoissonLaw(gamma; scale=1.0, npoints=1201)

The free Poisson law with parameter gamma - the Marcenko-Pastur law with ratio gamma, with the variable multiplied by scale. Support scale * (1 +- sqrt(gamma))^2, mean scale; for gamma > 1 there is an atom at 0 of mass 1 - 1/gamma. Its transforms (for scale = 1) are R(w) = 1 / (1 - gamma * w) and S(w) = 1 / (1 + gamma * w). The discretization uses a Chebyshev grid in the support variable, so the density spike at the lower edge for gamma near 1 (and the singular density at gamma = 1) is resolved accurately.

RandomMatrix.FreeBessel — Function
FreeBessel(s, t; npoints=2001)

The free Bessel law pi_{s,t} of Banica, Belinschi, Capitaine & Collins, "Free Bessel laws", Canad. J. Math. 63(1), 2011, pp. 3-37 (arXiv:0710.5931), as a FreeMeasure grid law: the density is computed from the algebraic equation for the moment series f = 1 + z f^s (f + t - 1) (their Proposition 4.1) on a grid over the support (freeBesselSupport), and the atom (1 - t)_+ at 0 is carried exactly in the atoms list. The grid is clustered at the lower edge (u^3 when the support touches 0, where the density diverges — like x^{-s/(s+1)} at t = 1 and like x^{-1/s} for t > 1 — and u^2 when it is bounded away from 0, where the density vanishes at both ends).

The parametrization is (s, t) as in the paper: s is the free multiplicative power (at integer s and t = 1, pi_{s,1} is the limiting spectral law of a product of s independent square Ginibre matrices, i.e. a product of s square Wishart matrices), and t is the mean — the compression trace in the Bernoulli-factor definition. It is deliberately not parametrized by a product depth and a Marcenko-Pastur ratio (k, gamma): a product of k >= 2 laws MP_gamma with gamma != 1 is pi_{s,t} for no (s, t) (the S-transform identity would force (t + z)(1 + gamma z)^k = (1 + z)^{s-1}, impossible for gamma != 1; the paper's Section 5). The boundary dictionaries are: pi_{1,t} is the dilation by t of MP_{1/t} (see freePoissonLaw), and pi_{s,1} is the s-fold free multiplicative convolution of MP_1.

Domain: (s, t) in (0, Inf)^2 minus the critical rectangle (0, 1) x (1, Inf) of the paper's Definition 1.4; the constructor throws ArgumentError there rather than extrapolating the equation into a region where the law may not exist.

API shape: the result is a FreeMeasure, not a Distributions law — the density has no elementary closed form, only the algebraic equation above, and the grid measure is the package's working representation for such laws (freeMean, freeVar, freeDensity, freeAtoms, cauchyTransform and the free convolutions all apply). Exact moments are available separately from freeBesselMoments, support and atom from freeBesselSupport.

Accuracy, calibrated against the paper's anchors before the test bands were set: freeBesselMoments agrees with the independent recursion from f = 1 + z f^s (f + t - 1) to 0 at integer cases and < 2e-12 at non-integer ones (k <= 10); the density reproduces the exact moments to a worst relative error of 0.0069% in quadrature before normalization; and the returned measure's first four moments match the exact ones to at most about 0.13% at the default resolution across the calibrated cases (2, 1), (3, 1), (2, 1/2), (2, 2), (2, 3), (3/2, 2) — the worst case is (3, 1), whose x^{-3/4} edge singularity is the hardest to weight. The singular-edge rates check against the paper's sharp constants: at s = 2, t > 1, pi rho(x) sqrt(x) -> 1/sqrt(t - 1) (0.707 at t = 3, read off the grid sweep to three digits).

Examples

fb = FreeBessel(2, 1)          # product of two square Wisharts' limit
freeMean(fb)                   # 1 = t
freeAtoms(FreeBessel(2, 0.5))  # [0.0 => 0.5]
freeBesselMoments(2, 1, 4)     # [1, 3, 12, 55]  (Fuss-Catalan)

RandomMatrix.freeBesselMoments — Function
freeBesselMoments(s, t, kmax)

The first kmax moments m_1, ..., m_kmax of the free Bessel law pi_{s,t}, exactly, by the Fuss-Narayana formula (Banica-Belinschi- Capitaine-Collins, Theorem 5.2)

\[m_k = \sum_{b=1}^{k} \frac{1}{b} \binom{k-1}{b-1} \binom{s k}{b-1} t^b\]

with the generalized binomial coefficient for real s. At t = 1 these are the Fuss-Catalan numbers 1/(s k + 1) * binomial(s k + k, k) — at s = 2: 1, 3, 12, 55, 273; at s = 3: 1, 4, 22, 140, 969 — and m_1 = t always.

source
RandomMatrix.freeBesselSupport — Function
freeBesselSupport(s, t)

The support of the free Bessel law pi_{s,t} as a named tuple (lo, hi, atom): the absolutely continuous part lives on [lo, hi] and atom is the mass of the atom at 0. For t < 1 the atom is 1 - t and the interval is bounded away from 0 (Banica-Belinschi- Capitaine-Collins, Theorem 3.1); for t = 1 the support is [0, (s+1)^(s+1)/s^s] (Theorem 2.1); for t > 1 there is no atom and the support is [0, K_+] with K_+ from Theorem 3.2 — except at s = 1, where the law is the free Poisson law of parameter t and the lower edge is (1 - sqrt(t))^2.

sTransform(mu, w)

RandomMatrix.freeMul — Function
freeMul(mu, nu; npoints=1001, eps=nothing)

The free multiplicative convolution mu boxtimes nu for measures on [0, infinity), via the S-transform product rule with nested Newton inversion. Atoms: at 0 the mass is max(mu({0}), nu({0})); elsewhere the sum-minus-one rule at products of atoms. The output grid is a Chebyshev grid, clustered at the support ends, and eps (the Stieltjes regularization) defaults to half the mean grid spacing.

Accuracy, measured against the exact moments of MP_gamma boxtimes MP_delta (from the S-transform) at the default resolution: when both factors are bounded away from 0 the first three moments err by at most about 2.5%. When a factor's support touches 0 the product density diverges at 0, the eps-smoothing leaks mass below 0 at a rate ~ eps^(1/3), and the moment errors grow to about 5% (MP_1 boxtimes MP_0.5) and 11% (MP_1 boxtimes MP_1). Passing a smaller eps (down to about 1e-4 times the support scale) improves the cases bounded away from 0 to ~0.05% and MP_1 boxtimes MP_0.5 to ~0.6%, but for measures with an atom at 0, very small eps makes the Newton continuation fail at many points near the lower edge (and the run slows down tenfold) — keep the default there.

RandomMatrix.freeCompress — Function
freeCompress(mu, tau; npoints=1201, eps=nothing)

The free compression of mu by a factor tau in (0, 1]: the law of p a p in the compressed space (p A p, phi/tau), where a has law mu and p is a projection of trace tau free from a. This is the compression of Nica & Speicher, Lectures on the Combinatorics of Free Probability, Cambridge University Press, 2006. Note the normalization: the trace on the compressed space is renormalized by 1/tau, so the mean is preserved. (The law of p a p under the original trace is the different measure (1 - tau) delta_0 + tau * mu_tau, and some authors parametrize the same family by 1/tau >= 1, as a decompression.)

Characterizing identities: the R-transform scales as R_{mu_tau}(w) = R_mu(tau * w), i.e. the free cumulants scale as kappa_n -> tau^(n-1) kappa_n; an atom of mu at a of mass m survives exactly when m > 1 - tau, with compressed mass (m - (1 - tau)) / tau. Consequences: a semicircle of variance sigma^2 compresses to a semicircle of variance tau * sigma^2, and the free Poisson (Marcenko-Pastur) law MP_gamma compresses to MP_{tau * gamma} — its atom at 0 (mass 1 - 1/gamma for gamma > 1) disappears exactly when tau * gamma <= 1. For a sample covariance matrix this is the limiting spectral law of a principal submatrix retaining a tau fraction of the coordinates.

Computation: the Cauchy transform of the result is evaluated through the implicit equation G_{mu_tau}(z) = G_mu(s) / tau, where s solves s - (1 - tau) / G_mu(s) = z (Newton iteration on the exact rational G_mu, with continuation along the grid), the density follows by Stieltjes inversion, and the atoms are inserted exactly. eps is the Stieltjes regularization (default: the grid spacing). tau == 1 returns mu unchanged.

source
RandomMatrix.silversteinLaw — Function
silversteinLaw(points, masses, gamma; npoints=2001, eps=nothing)
silversteinLaw(mu::FreeMeasure, gamma; npoints=2001, eps=nothing)
silversteinLaw(d::UnivariateDistribution, gamma; hpoints=401, npoints=2001, eps=nothing)

The limiting spectral distribution F of the sample covariance matrix $(1/n) T^{1/2} X X' T^{1/2}$, where X is p x n with i.i.d. entries of mean 0 and variance 1, T is a deterministic positive semidefinite population covariance whose spectral distribution is H, and gamma = p/n. Where MarchenkoPastur covers the identity population (H a point mass at 1), this is the law for a general population.

F is defined only implicitly, through the Silverstein equation (Silverstein & Choi, "Analysis of the limiting spectral distribution of large dimensional random matrices", J. Multivariate Anal. 54(2), 1995, Eq. (1.3); Bai & Silverstein, Spectral Analysis of Large Dimensional Random Matrices, Springer, 2010, Ch. 4): the companion Stieltjes transform $\underline m(z) = \int (t - z)^{-1} d\underline F(t)$ of the Gram matrix's limiting law is the unique solution in the upper half plane of

\[\underline m = -\Big(z - \gamma \int \frac{t}{1 + t \underline m} \, dH(t)\Big)^{-1}, \qquad \underline m(z) = -\frac{1 - \gamma}{z} + \gamma \, m_F(z),\]

and the density of F is $\rho(x) = \operatorname{Im} m_F(x + i0)/\pi$. The solver evaluates $\underline m$ by Newton iteration with continuation along the grid, recovers m_F from the displayed relation, and reads the density off by Stieltjes inversion; the grid runs from 0 to the largest support edge, the edges being the critical values of the inverse map $z(m) = \gamma \int t/(1 + tm) dH(t) - 1/m$. The grid is a Chebyshev grid, clustered at the ends (a support edge at 0 carries an $x^{-1/2}$-diverging density, which a uniform grid misweights, as in freePoissonLaw). eps is the Stieltjes regularization (default: a fifth of the mean grid spacing).

The atom at 0 is exact: its mass is $\max(H(\{0\}), 1 - 1/\gamma)$ – the rank deficiency of the sample covariance, whichever of T and the sampling rectangle causes more of it. The moments are exact in the limit: $\int x dF = \int t dH$ and $\int x^2 dF = \int t^2 dH + \gamma (\int t dH)^2$.

API shape: the result is a FreeMeasure, not a Distributions law – like the free convolutions, this law has no closed-form density, cdf, or sampler, and the grid measure is the package's working representation for such laws (freeMean, freeVar, freeDensity, freeAtoms and cauchyTransform all apply). H is taken primarily as discrete points/masses, where the Silverstein integral is an exact finite sum; a FreeMeasure H contributes its nodes and weights; a Distributions law H is first discretized on an equal-weight quantile grid of hpoints points, an approximation that converges as hpoints grows (the law must be supported on [0, Inf), as T is positive semidefinite).

Accuracy, calibrated against the exact anchors before the test bands were set: at H = delta_1 the support edges agree with MarchenkoPastur exactly and the density reproduces it to about 1e-3 in the interior (7e-3 within a smoothing width of an edge); at the default resolution the first two moments of the returned measure match the exact formulas above to within 0.3% across single-mass, two-mass, and three-mass populations, with and without an atom at 0 – the worst case is H = delta_1 at gamma = 1, whose density diverges at the lower edge 0 (mean within 0.9%). A Distributions population adds its quantile-discretization error (0.2% on the mean for Gamma(2, 1.5) at the default hpoints).

# [2.485, 6.052]; at p/n = 0.5 it is a single interval.

freeMean(F)   # 2.5 = mean of H
freeAtoms(F)  # no atom: gamma < 1 and H has no atom at 0

RandomMatrix.profileWignerLaw — Function
profileWignerLaw(S; npoints=2001, eps=nothing)
profileWignerLaw(sizes, B; npoints=2001, eps=nothing)

The limiting spectral distribution of a Wigner matrix with variance profile S: an n x n Hermitian matrix whose entries are independent (up to the symmetry) with

\[\mathbb{E}\,|X_{ij}|^2 = S_{ij} / n,\]

in the package's Wigner normalization (the constant profile S = sigma^2 ones is sigma * rand(GOE(n), norm=true), whose law is semicircleLaw(sigma)). The limit is characterized by the quadratic vector equation (Ajanki, Erdos & Kruger, "Quadratic vector equations on complex upper half-plane", Probab. Theory Related Fields 170 (2018), 683-747; arXiv:1506.05095): the vector Stieltjes transform m(z) is the unique solution in the upper half plane of

\[-\frac{1}{m_i(z)} = z + \frac{1}{n} \sum_j S_{ij}\, m_j(z), \qquad \rho(x) = \frac{1}{\pi n} \sum_i \operatorname{Im} m_i(x + i0),\]

and this function evaluates that density by Stieltjes inversion on a Chebyshev grid and returns it as a FreeMeasure — like silversteinLaw, this law has no closed-form density, cdf, or sampler, and the grid measure is the package's working representation for such laws (freeMean, freeVar, freeDensity, freeAtoms and cauchyTransform all apply). The grid covers [-B, B] (plus a 4% pad), where B = 2 sqrt(max_i (1/n) sum_j S_ij) bounds the support. eps is the Stieltjes regularization (default: a twentieth of the mean grid spacing). The support, the density, and the edges need not be symmetric-interval or semicircle-like: block profiles produce multi-cluster laws and cusps, all read off the same solve.

Two exact reductions anchor the solver. The constant profile collapses to the scalar equation sigma^2 m^2 + z m + 1 = 0, i.e. semicircleLaw(sigma). A block-diagonal profile is the exact mixture of the block semicircles — with the full-size normalization above, a diagonal block of variance b covering a fraction p of the coordinates contributes a semicircle of radius 2 sqrt(b p) with weight p. Coordinates whose profile row is identically zero are exact zero eigenvalues: they are removed from the solve and returned as an atom at 0 of mass (zero rows)/n (see freeAtoms); the all-zero profile is diracLaw(0).

The block form profileWignerLaw(sizes, B) builds the block-constant profile whose (a, b) block (of sizes[a] x sizes[b] entries) is constantly B[a, b] and delegates to the matrix form. Identical rows of the profile share an m value by uniqueness of the QVE solution, so the solve runs on row groups: block profiles solve on the K x K block system exactly, at any block sizes, while a fully inhomogeneous profile of size n solves on its n groups (a few seconds at n = 400 at the default resolution; Newton for at most 16 groups, Anderson-accelerated fixed-point iteration above that, an eta-laddered fixed point as fallback).

The exact moments are int x dmu = 0 (the law is symmetric: odd moments of a centered Wigner matrix vanish for every profile) and int x^2 dmu = (1/n^2) sum_ij S_ij, the mean profile entry.

Accuracy, calibrated against the exact anchors and formed-matrix simulation before the test bands were set: the constant profile reproduces the semicircle density to 1.7e-5 (sup over the interior) at the default resolution, its second moment to 2.5e-5 (1.6e-6 and 2.8e-6 at eps = 1e-5); the block-diagonal mixture matches its exact density to 3.3e-4 and its moments to 1e-4; solver residuals are at most 1e-12 (Newton, block/constant) and 2e-10 (Anderson, inhomogeneous). Against simulation of a two-block profile (B = [2 0.5; 0.5 1], equal blocks) the law's CDF matches the pooled empirical CDF to 0.001 at n = 400, improving with n, and the predicted support edge 2.156 is approached by the simulated largest eigenvalue from below at the shallow-edge Tracy-Widom scale. randProfileWigner samples the ensemble itself.

Examples

# Two communities with within-variance 2 and 1, cross-variance 0.5:
# a single-cluster law of variance (2 + 1 + 2*0.5)/4 = 1.
mu = profileWignerLaw([100, 100], [2.0 0.5; 0.5 1.0])
freeMean(mu)   # 0
freeVar(mu)    # 1.0001 (exact: 1)

# A band profile: variance 1 within distance 20 of the diagonal.
n = 200
S = [abs(i - j) <= 20 ? 1.0 : 0.0 for i in 1:n, j in 1:n]
freeVar(profileWignerLaw(S))   # 0.19451 (exact value: mean(S) = 0.1945)

RandomMatrix.randomFeaturesLaw — Function
randomFeaturesLaw(mu0, mu1, muStar, psi, phi; npoints=2001, eps=nothing, hpoints=1201)
randomFeaturesLaw(sigma::Function, psi, phi; quadpoints=128, kwargs...)
randomFeaturesLaw(preset::Symbol, psi, phi; kwargs...)

The limiting spectral distribution of the random-features covariance matrix, as a FreeMeasure.

Model: let $W$ ($m \times d$) and $X$ ($d \times n$) have i.i.d. $N(0,1)$ entries and form the feature matrix $\Phi = \sigma(W X / \sqrt d)$ ($m \times n$; the preactivations are marginally $N(0,1)$). This is the Mei-Montanari random-features model (equivalently: data $x_j \sim N(0, I_d/d)$ and weights $\theta_i \sim N(0, I_d)$ with $\Phi_{ij} = \sigma(\theta_i \cdot x_j)$). The object returned is the limiting law of the feature covariance $C = \Phi \Phi' / n$ ($m \times m$). The sample Gram $\Phi' \Phi / n$ ($n \times n$) has the same nonzero spectrum; its limiting law is the same continuous part rescaled by $m/n$, with an additional atom at 0 of mass $(1 - m/n)_+$.

With the Hermite coefficients of sigma for $Z \sim N(0,1)$ (hermiteCoefficients)

\[\mu_0 = E\,\sigma(Z), \qquad \mu_1 = E[Z\sigma(Z)], \qquad \mu_\star^2 = E\,\sigma(Z)^2 - \mu_0^2 - \mu_1^2,\]

and aspect ratios $\psi = n/d$ (samples per dimension) and $\phi = m/d$ (features per dimension), the bulk law depends on sigma only through (mu1, muStar): it is the Silverstein law (silversteinLaw) with population

\[H = \mathrm{law}\big(\mu_1^2 T + \mu_\star^2\big), \qquad T \sim MP_{m/d},\]

at aspect $\gamma = m/n = \phi/\psi$. Derivation: linearizing $\sigma$ at the Hermite level, $\Phi \approx \mu_0 \mathbf 1 \mathbf 1' + \mu_1 W X/\sqrt d + \tilde\Phi$ with $\tilde\Phi$ spectrally equivalent to an i.i.d. Gaussian matrix of variance $\mu_\star^2$ (the Gaussian equivalence of Mei & Montanari); conditionally on $W$ the columns are then i.i.d. with population covariance $\mu_1^2 W W'/d + \mu_\star^2 I$, whose spectral distribution is $H$, and the column sampling is Silverstein's. Substituting the Marchenko-Pastur quadratic for $H$'s transform into the Silverstein equation yields the quartic self-consistent equation for the Stieltjes transform found by Pennington & Worah (2017) (see also Benigni & Peche, 2021; Schroeder & Piccolo, 2021, Thm. 2.2, whose $\theta_1 = E\sigma(Z)^2$, $\theta_2 = (E\sigma'(Z))^2 = \mu_1^2$ are the same two parameters).

Exact reductions and moments:

  • $\mu_1 = 0$: $H$ collapses to a point mass at $\mu_\star^2$ and the law is exactly the Marchenko-Pastur law $MP_{m/n}$ scaled by $\mu_\star^2$ – the Pennington-Worah isospectral case $\theta_2 = 0$.
  • linear sigma ($\mu_1 = 1$, $\mu_\star = 0$): the law of a product of two Wishart matrices, $MP_{m/d} \boxtimes MP_{m/n}$ (the quartic degenerates to a cubic).
  • the mean is $\mu_1^2 + \mu_\star^2$ and the second moment $\mu_1^4 (1 + m/d) + 2\mu_1^2\mu_\star^2 + \mu_\star^4 + (m/n)(\mu_1^2 + \mu_\star^2)^2$ (Silverstein's moment formulas applied to $H$).
  • atom at 0 of mass $\max(H(\{0\}), 1 - n/m)$, with $H(\{0\}) = (1 - d/m)_+$ when $\mu_\star = 0$ and 0 otherwise: the rank deficiency of $C$, whichever of the two rectangles causes more of it.

mu0 does not enter the bulk law. It produces a single outlier eigenvalue of $C$ at $\mu_0^2 m (1 + o(1))$ (a rank-one mean component; measured at $1.014$–$1.019$ times $\mu_0^2 m$ for $m = 300$–$375$ in the calibration simulations), which the limiting law does not see; simulations comparing against this law should drop the top eigenvalue when mu0 != 0, exactly as for the trivial eigenvalue of a regular graph in the Kesten-McKay law.

The sigma::Function method computes the Hermite coefficients by Gauss-Hermite quadrature (quadpoints nodes). The preset::Symbol method accepts :linear, :relu, :erf and :quadratic, whose coefficients are the closed forms in hermiteCoefficients.

Accuracy: inherited from silversteinLaw (density to about 1e-3 in the interior). At the default Stieltjes regularization the first two moments of the composed law carry a positive bias of up to about 1.5% (the population's own discretization enters through hpoints); passing a smaller eps (e.g. eps = 5e-5) reduces the moment bias to about 0.1%. The mu1 = 0 reduction to Marchenko-Pastur is accurate to the silversteinLaw level (moments to ~0.1%, interior density to ~2e-3). Calibrated against formed-matrix simulation at (d, n, m) = (300, 450, 375): the empirical spectral moments of ReLU features (top eigenvalue dropped) and of erf features agree with the exact moment formulas to within simulation error (rep-to-rep sd), the bulk CDF distance is about 0.01, and the ReLU outlier sits at 1.014–1.019 times mu0^2 m. As with silversteinLaw, this law has no closed-form density, cdf or sampler; the grid FreeMeasure is the working representation (freeMean, freeVar, freeDensity, freeAtoms, cauchyTransform all apply).

Examples

freeMean(F)   # approx 0.343

RandomMatrix.hermiteCoefficients — Function
hermiteCoefficients(sigma; npoints=128) -> (mu0, mu1, muStar)

The first Hermite coefficients of an activation sigma for Z ~ N(0, 1):

\[\mu_0 = E\,\sigma(Z), \qquad \mu_1 = E[Z \sigma(Z)] = E\,\sigma'(Z), \qquad \mu_\star^2 = E\,\sigma(Z)^2 - \mu_0^2 - \mu_1^2 \ge 0,\]

(the middle identity is Stein's lemma). These are the only features of sigma that the limiting spectral law of a random-features Gram matrix sees; see randomFeaturesLaw. The expectations are evaluated by Gauss-Hermite quadrature with npoints nodes (Golub-Welsch on the probabilists' Hermite recurrence). Smooth activations converge spectrally (machine precision well before 128 nodes for tanh, erf, polynomials); a kink converges algebraically – for ReLU the mu0/muStar error is about 1.3e-3 at the default 128 nodes and halves as npoints doubles, while mu1 is exact to machine precision. For ReLU prefer the :relu preset of randomFeaturesLaw, which uses the closed form.

Closed forms, for reference (all for Z ~ N(0, 1)):

  • linear, sigma(x) = x: (0, 1, 0).
  • ReLU, sigma(x) = max(x, 0): mu0 = 1/sqrt(2 pi) (the Gaussian density at 0), mu1 = E[Z^2 1{Z>0}] = 1/2, E sigma^2 = 1/2, hence mu*^2 = 1/4 - 1/(2 pi).
  • erf: mu0 = 0 (odd), mu1 = E sigma'(Z) = (2/sqrt(pi)) E exp(-Z^2) = 2/sqrt(3 pi), and E erf(Z)^2 = (2/pi) asin(2/3) (from E[erf(aZ) erf(bZ)] = (2/pi) asin(2ab / sqrt((1+2a^2) (1+2b^2)))), hence mu*^2 = (2/pi) asin(2/3) - 4/(3 pi).
  • quadratic, sigma(x) = (x^2 - 1)/sqrt(2): (0, 0, 1) – the normalized second Hermite polynomial itself.

RandomMatrix.BrownMeasure — Type
BrownMeasure(inner, outer, atom0, radius, cdf, density)

The Brown measure of an R-diagonal element, as returned by brownMeasure: a rotation-invariant probability measure on the complex plane, stored through its radial law.

  • inner, outer: the support is the annulus inner <= |z| <= outer (a disk when inner == 0); the radii are exact, outer = sqrt(int t dnu) and inner = sqrt(1 / int t^{-1} dnu) (0 when nu has an atom at 0).
  • atom0: the atom at z = 0, of mass exactly nu({0}).
  • radius, cdf: the radial CDF Phi(r) = mu({|z| <= r}) on an increasing grid from inner to outer, endpoints included; Phi already accounts for the atom (Phi(inner) = atom0 up to the continuous part starting there).
  • density: the planar density rho(z) = Phi'(|z|) / (2 pi |z|) at the grid radii (interior values are the closed-form Haagerup–Larsen derivative; the two endpoint values repeat the nearest interior value). Empty when the measure is singular (a single circle: nu = delta_c), where no planar density exists.

RandomMatrix.brownMeasure — Function
brownMeasure(points, masses; npoints=2001)
brownMeasure(mu::FreeMeasure; npoints=2001)
brownMeasure(d::UnivariateDistribution; hpoints=401, npoints=2001)

The Brown measure of an R-diagonal element a whose squared modulus a* a has law nu — the two-dimensional limiting eigenvalue distribution of the matrix model A = U P with U Haar unitary and P positive with spectral law nu (more generally A = U T V, the single ring theorem of Guionnet, Krishnapur & Zeitouni, Ann. of Math. 174(2) (2011), 1189–1217). This is the package's first non-Hermitian limiting law: the measure lives on the complex plane, is rotation invariant, and is returned as a BrownMeasure (radii, radial CDF and planar density on a grid), not as a FreeMeasure — a real-line grid law cannot represent it.

By Haagerup & Larsen (J. Funct. Anal. 176 (2000), 331–367, Thm. 4.4, Cor. 4.5), with S_nu the S-transform of nu, the radial CDF Phi(r) = mu_a({|z| <= r}) is determined by

\[\mu_a\big(B(0, S_\nu(t-1)^{-1/2})\big) = t, \qquad t \in (\nu(\{0\}), 1],\]

the support is the annulus (single ring) with exact radii outer = sqrt(int t dnu) and inner = sqrt(1 / int t^{-1} dnu), and mu_a has an atom at 0 of mass exactly nu({0}). Boundary behaviour, all exact: an atom nu({0}) = p_0 > 0 gives the atom at 0 and forces inner = 0 (the continuous part fills the disk); int t^{-1} dnu = Inf (e.g. nu with a density at 0) also gives inner = 0; nu = delta_c gives the uniform measure on the circle of radius sqrt(c) (singular: the returned density grid is empty); nu = delta_0 gives delta_0. When inner = 0 and nu has a density at 0 the planar density stays finite at the centre (for the circular element it is the constant 1/pi); for an atom-carrying nu it can diverge at the outer edge instead (Haagerup–Larsen's Bernoulli example below).

The solver parametrizes the Haagerup–Larsen relation by s in (0, Inf) through the moment transform of the discrete nu: with N(s) = sum_j w_j s t_j / (1 + s t_j) and u = 1 - N(s), the pair (r, Phi(r)) = (sqrt((1-u)/(s u)), u) traces the radial CDF exactly, and the planar density follows in closed form by differentiating the parametrization. The grid is cosine-clustered in the mass coordinate u (npoints interior points); nu is taken primarily as discrete points/masses, where N(s) is an exact finite sum; a FreeMeasure contributes its nodes and weights; a Distributions law is first discretized on an equal-weight quantile grid of hpoints points (an approximation that converges as hpoints grows; the law must be supported on [0, Inf)).

Accuracy, calibrated against the primary sources' own worked examples before the test bands were set (calibration scripts hidden_files/brown_proto.jl, brown_calib.jl): the circular element (nu the free Poisson law MP_1, Haagerup–Larsen Ex. 5.1) returns Phi(r) = r^2 to about 1e-7 and density 1/pi to 1e-14 on the bulk of the disk; within r <~ 0.05 of the centre its density instead reflects the input grid law's own discreteness near t = 0 (the freePoissonLaw grid carries a node at 0 of weight 8e-4, and the solver is faithful to the law it is given); the free Poisson family nu_c (Ex. 5.2) returns density exactly 1/pi on its annulus [sqrt(c-1), sqrt(c)] (atom 1 - c at 0 and disk when c < 1); the Bernoulli law nu = (1-a) delta_0 + a delta_1 (Ex. 5.3) returns the density (1-a) / (pi (1-r^2)^2) on the disk of radius sqrt(a) to 1e-4 (worst at the edge, 1e-8 inside); a two-point nu reproduces the exact radii and, through int |z|^{2k} dmu_a = int_0^1 S_nu(u-1)^{-k} du, the S-transform moment identity to about 1e-4. Formed-matrix simulation (A = U P, Haar U from this package) at n = 500 matches the radial CDF with pooled KS distance about 0.03 — the deviation sits in the finite-n edge layers (eigenvalue extremes overshoot the outer radius by about 2% at that size), not in the bulk.

Examples

# exactly sqrt(2).

(bm.inner, bm.outer)   # (1.2649..., 1.5811...)
radialCDF(bm, sqrt(2.0))   # 0.5

# The circular element: nu = MP_1, the uniform unit disk.

radialCDF(bm, 0.5)     # 0.25 = 0.5^2
brownDensity(bm, 0.5)  # 0.3183... = 1/pi
