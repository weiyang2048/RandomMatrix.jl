Free Probability

Free additive ($\boxplus$) and multiplicative ($\boxtimes$) convolution of probability measures, computed numerically. A FreeMeasure is a discrete representation (nodes and weights) of a probability measure; the Cauchy, R and S transforms are then exact rational functions, and the convolutions are evaluated by the subordination fixed point (additive case) and the S-transform product rule (multiplicative case). Atoms of a convolution follow the exact Bercovici–Voiculescu rules.

Accuracy notes

freePoissonLaw is discretized on a Chebyshev grid, which resolves the density spike at the lower Marčenko–Pastur edge for gamma near 1 (on a uniform grid the mean came out 2.3% high at gamma = 0.99, and at gamma = 1 the density at 0 is NaN, which poisoned every weight); its mean and variance now match 1 and gamma to about 1e-7.

For freeMul, measured against the exact moments of MP_gamma ⊠ MP_delta (from the S-transform) at the default resolution: with both factors bounded away from 0, the first three moments err by at most about 2.5%. When a factor's support touches 0, the product density diverges at 0, the Stieltjes smoothing (eps, default half the mean grid spacing) leaks mass below 0 at a rate ~ eps^(1/3), and the errors grow to about 5% (MP_1 ⊠ MP_0.5) and 11% (MP_1 ⊠ MP_1). A smaller eps — down to about 1e-4 times the support scale — improves the atom-free cases substantially, but is not recommended for measures with an atom at 0, where the Newton continuation fails near the lower edge.

Worked example

The free Poisson (Marčenko–Pastur) law has mean $1$ and variance $\gamma$, reproduced here to the accuracy quoted above; the free multiplicative convolution of two such laws has exact mean $1 \cdot 1 = 1$ and exact second moment $1 + \gamma + \delta$ (S-transform), hence variance $\gamma + \delta = 0.5$ for $\gamma = \delta = 0.25$ — the numerical convolution lands within about 1% of both, as expected for factors bounded away from 0.

using RandomMatrix
fp = freePoissonLaw(0.5)
(mean = round(freeMean(fp), digits = 6), var = round(freeVar(fp), digits = 6))
(mean = 1.0, var = 0.5)
prod = freeMul(freePoissonLaw(0.25), freePoissonLaw(0.25))
(mean = round(freeMean(prod), digits = 3), var = round(freeVar(prod), digits = 3))
(mean = 1.002, var = 0.503)

Free additive convolution of two semicircles of variance 1 is a semicircle of variance 2 (the R-transforms add):

ss = freeAdd(semicircleLaw(1.0), semicircleLaw(1.0))
(mean = round(freeMean(ss), digits = 3), var = round(freeVar(ss), digits = 3))
(mean = -0.0, var = 2.015)
RandomMatrix.FreeMeasure — Type
FreeMeasure(nodes, weights, atoms = Pair{Float64,Float64}[])

A probability measure represented by discrete quadrature nodes and weights (summing to the mass of the absolutely continuous part) plus a list of atoms (location => mass). This is the working representation for the free-probability transforms and convolutions: on it, the Cauchy transform is an exact rational function.

RandomMatrix.semicircleLaw — Function
semicircleLaw(sigma=1.0; center=0.0, npoints=801)

The semicircle law with variance sigma^2, as a FreeMeasure: density sqrt(4 sigma^2 - (x-center)^2) / (2 pi sigma^2) on [center - 2 sigma, center + 2 sigma]. Its R-transform is R(w) = sigma^2 w.

source
RandomMatrix.freePoissonLaw — Function
freePoissonLaw(gamma; scale=1.0, npoints=1201)

The free Poisson law with parameter gamma - the Marcenko-Pastur law with ratio gamma, with the variable multiplied by scale. Support scale * (1 +- sqrt(gamma))^2, mean scale; for gamma > 1 there is an atom at 0 of mass 1 - 1/gamma. Its transforms (for scale = 1) are R(w) = 1 / (1 - gamma * w) and S(w) = 1 / (1 + gamma * w). The discretization uses a Chebyshev grid in the support variable, so the density spike at the lower edge for gamma near 1 (and the singular density at gamma = 1) is resolved accurately.

RandomMatrix.cauchyTransform — Function
cauchyTransform(mu, z)

The Cauchy (Stieltjes) transform G_mu(z) = int d mu(t) / (z - t), evaluated exactly for the discrete representation of mu.

source
RandomMatrix.rTransform — Function
rTransform(mu, w)

The R-transform of mu at w: R(w) = K(w) - 1/w where K is the functional inverse of the Cauchy transform. R linearizes free additive convolution: R_{mu boxplus nu} = R_mu + R_nu. Computed by Newton inversion of G; accurate for w in a neighbourhood of 0 whose size is set by the support of mu.

source
RandomMatrix.sTransform — Function
sTransform(mu, w)

The S-transform of mu at w: S(w) = (1 + w)/w * chi(w) where chi is the functional inverse of psi(z) = int z t / (1 - z t) d mu(t). S linearizes free multiplicative convolution: S_{mu boxtimes nu} = S_mu * S_nu. Computed by Newton inversion of psi; requires freeMean(mu) != 0.

source
RandomMatrix.freeAdd — Function
freeAdd(mu, nu; npoints=1201, eps=nothing)

The free additive convolution mu boxplus nu. The density is computed by the subordination fixed point on a grid covering the Minkowski sum of the supports; atoms follow the exact Bercovici-Voiculescu rule. eps is the Stieltjes regularization (default: the grid spacing).

source
RandomMatrix.freeMul — Function
freeMul(mu, nu; npoints=1001, eps=nothing)

The free multiplicative convolution mu boxtimes nu for measures on [0, infinity), via the S-transform product rule with nested Newton inversion. Atoms: at 0 the mass is max(mu({0}), nu({0})); elsewhere the sum-minus-one rule at products of atoms. The output grid is a Chebyshev grid, clustered at the support ends, and eps (the Stieltjes regularization) defaults to half the mean grid spacing.

Accuracy, measured against the exact moments of MP_gamma boxtimes MP_delta (from the S-transform) at the default resolution: when both factors are bounded away from 0 the first three moments err by at most about 2.5%. When a factor's support touches 0 the product density diverges at 0, the eps-smoothing leaks mass below 0 at a rate ~ eps^(1/3), and the moment errors grow to about 5% (MP_1 boxtimes MP_0.5) and 11% (MP_1 boxtimes MP_1). Passing a smaller eps (down to about 1e-4 times the support scale) improves the cases bounded away from 0 to ~0.05% and MP_1 boxtimes MP_0.5 to ~0.6%, but for measures with an atom at 0, very small eps makes the Newton continuation fail at many points near the lower edge (and the run slows down tenfold) — keep the default there.

RandomMatrix.freeDensity — Function
freeDensity(mu, xs; eps=...)

The (absolutely continuous) density of mu at the points xs, from Stieltjes inversion: rho(x) = -(1/pi) Im G_mu(x + i eps). Atoms appear as Lorentzian bumps of width eps; use freeAtoms for their exact locations and masses.
